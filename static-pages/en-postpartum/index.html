Before you start: pelvic floor rehabilitation

The pelvic floor is the foundation for everything else. Your pelvic floor rehabilitation must have started, with your midwife or a physical therapist trained in pelvic health. Strengthening the abs on a pelvic floor that isn't ready yet can cause or worsen leaks and a feeling of heaviness.

What is diastasis recti?

During pregnancy, the two rectus abdominis muscles move apart to make room for the baby. This is normal. In some women, this gap persists after giving birth: a rounded belly, a feeling of abdominal weakness, back pain.

At the assessment, I measure your diastasis and assess how your abdominal wall works, so I can adapt the exercises.

Surgery is sometimes considered, often as part of a tummy tuck: I support you before and after the operation.

My approach: the whole body

Pregnancy and childbirth change your postural balance: for nine months, your pelvis, back, breathing muscles, rib cage and abs have adapted, as have the muscles in your legs. That's why I offer whole-body care, so you can get back to your activities with confidence, as quickly as your body allows.

Elite sport taught me that a comeback is prepared step by step, with clear milestones. Being a mother and an athlete taught me everything else: the tiredness, the lack of time, the wish to get your body back. I bring these two experiences together to support you.

How does the program work?

  1. 1

    The assessment

    Pregnancy, childbirth, fitness level, tiredness, breastfeeding. I assess posture, breathing, abdominal wall and diastasis.

  2. 2

    Reconnect

    Breathing, posture, everyday movements (carrying your baby, breastfeeding), first gentle exercises.

  3. 3

    Rebuild

    Progressive, whole-body strengthening: hypopressives, Pilates, core work, back, pelvis, legs.

  4. 4

    Return to sport

    Running, team sports, fitness, dance… with clear steps so you know when to move forward.
